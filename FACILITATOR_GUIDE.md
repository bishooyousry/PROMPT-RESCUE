# Facilitator guide — PROMPT RESCUE: The AI Emergency Room (online)

**Goal:** participants can spot what is missing in a weak prompt and rewrite it with CRAFT (Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone).
**Original activity:** *Prompt Makeover* — weak prompt “Write a report.” (classroom: pairs, 4 minutes). **Online version:** individual, 8–10 minutes of play + 5–7 minutes debrief.

## Before the session (5 min)
- Open the link → Trainer Dashboard (PIN 2026). One session per group (**+ New session**; name them).
- Put the **player link** (or the 5-character code) in the chat. An Arabic link is available.
- Tell participants: sound is optional (🔊), it works on phones, all data is fictional, and **never paste confidential data into any AI tool**.

## Run (timing)
| Time | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Say: “Here is a real weak prompt someone typed into AI: ‘Write a report.’ It is missing almost everything. Your job is to rescue it.” Share link/code. |
| 0:01 | Everyone is in. Optionally open **Projector view**. |
| 0:01–0:10 | Watch the table: phase, diagnosis ✓/✗ chips, health, hints, score. Expand a row (⌄) to read a final prompt. |
| ~0:09 | When most are done: **Reveal leaderboard** (players see it automatically) or use Projector view. |
| 0:10–0:17 | Debrief (below). |

If someone is stuck: “What would you tell a new employee who got this task with no other information?”

## What happens in the game (for narration)
1. **Failure** — the vague instruction produces a generic, useless (simulated) answer.
2. **Scan** — six sensors; players decide OK / WEAK / MISSING. Two traps on purpose: *Action* is WEAK (there is a verb but the task is vague) and *Polite words* is a false alarm (please/thank you are optional and add no information).
3. **Repair** — first clean the data (private customer details vs business numbers; placeholders like [CLIENT_A]); then rewrite the prompt while the health meter and vitals react. Hints cost coins.
4. **Before vs after** — comparison, a labelled simulated demonstration, and a real test in ChatGPT / Copilot / Gemini if your company allows it.
5. **Complete** — accuracy, repair completion, prompt, hints, coins, points.

## Debrief questions
1. **What exactly was missing from the weak prompt?** (Use the *Diagnosis — % correct on first filing* bars: low bars show what the group found hard to see.)
2. **Which CRAFT part do you personally forget most often?** (Use *CRAFT parts present in final prompts*: the lowest bar is the group's usual blind spot.)
3. Ask 2 volunteers to read their **before** and **after** prompt aloud (use ⌄ on a row), plus one reflection: “What improved, and why?”
4. Why is “please / thank you” not one of the five parts? What information does the AI actually need?
5. What data from your own work must be anonymized before it goes into a prompt? What placeholders would you use?
6. How did the real AI answer compare with the simulated demonstration? What would you change in the prompt next?

## Notes
- The automatic checks are keyword rules: use them as feedback, not as a grade. A passing prompt can still be poor — the real test is the AI's answer.
- Sealing the data sheet is final by design (one decision, with an explanation of every mistake).
- If the live connection drops: **Refresh** on the dashboard rebuilds from the relay history; otherwise collect the backup files and use **Import**.
- Export CSV at the end if you want to keep the results.
